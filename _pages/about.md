I am a Researcher Associate in the Computer Science Department at Carnegie Mellon University, where I am the member of the leadership team at the [Technology for Effective and Efficient Learning (TEEL) Lab](https://teel.cs.cmu.edu/). I develop methods through which expert judgment can guide, evaluate, and revise the behavior of language models. My work on **interpretable natural language specifications**, such as annotation instructions and evaluation criteria, aims to support researchers and experts at scale while keeping interpretation under their control. Our recent [Spec Learning preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24004) studies how to derive such specifications from brief instructions and comparisons between model responses.

My research connects two themes:
- **AI for supporting professionals** - I develop and evaluate methods for [assigning meaning-based labels to legal texts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13906) and [expert-guided thematic analysis in empirical legal research](https://ebooks.iospress.nl/doi/10.3233/FAIA230965). For example, a judicial opinion may mention a person's nervousness but reject it as grounds for suspicion. Computational analysis needs to preserve that distinction. My work in education also examines how AI can help educators [create and evaluate learning content](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3636243.3636256).
- **AI for fairness and access** - I investigate computational support for [retrieving judicial explanations of statutory terms](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10506-021-09293-5) and AI-supported [learning environments](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3631802.3631830). My curriculum and workforce research aims to make computational skills accessible to learners with varied educational and professional backgrounds.
